The Sawyerville–Eoline tornado produced severe and varied impacts along its multi-county path, including extensive structural and vegetation damage and multiple casualties. The NWS Birmingham damage survey and the Alabama Tornado Database both attribute seven fatalities and fifty injuries to this tornado, distributed among Greene, Hale, and Bibb Counties.[1][2] The NWS overview of the "Historic Outbreak of April 27, 2011" also lists these casualty figures for the event in its tabular summary of the day’s tornadoes.[9]

In Greene County, the tornado initially struck rural areas near the Tombigbee River just west of Tishabee, moving northeast across sparsely populated terrain north of Forkland.[2] The survey notes that at least four mobile homes, three outbuildings, and two churches were damaged or destroyed in this segment of the track, and two minor injuries were reported.[2] Although the early portion of the path affected fewer structures than later segments, the damage to small community facilities and residences in Greene County illustrates the breadth of impacts even at lower population density.[2]

Upon crossing the Black Warrior River into Hale County, west of Sawyerville, the tornado encountered more developed areas and intensified as it crossed Alabama Route 14 north of Sawyerville, reaching EF3 strength with estimated winds near 145 mph.[2] In this region, the tornado passed over and near local roads including County Roads 18, 21, and 29 and Alabama Highway 69 south of Harper Hill, generating extensive structural damage.[2] The NWS survey reports that at least forty injuries and six fatalities occurred in Hale County as homes and other buildings were destroyed or heavily damaged along this part of the track.[2] Thousands of trees were also uprooted or snapped, reflecting the strength and breadth of the circulation.[2]

The tornado’s impacts continued into Bibb County, where it moved through the Talladega National Forest near Ingram and then into the Eoline area.[2] In forested sections, the survey describes significant tree damage, while in Eoline the tornado again produced EF3-level destruction, with winds estimated near 145 mph.[2] Numerous mobile homes and single-family houses in Eoline sustained damage ranging from minor to major, and about a dozen such structures were destroyed outright.[2] Community infrastructure in Eoline was also affected: the Eoline Fire Department building and another nearby business were destroyed when the tornado passed through the area.[2] The survey documents one fatality in a vehicle near the fire department and at least ten other injuries in Bibb County, underscoring the localized severity of the event.[2]

Farther along its path, as the tornado crossed Alabama Highway 5 south of West Blocton, it weakened to EF2 intensity with estimated winds near 125 mph but continued to inflict major damage on several mobile homes and single-family residences near County Road 26.[2] Thousands of additional trees were downed along the later portion of the track before the tornado lifted just northeast of Marvel, at the northeastern end of its surveyed path.[2] Across all three counties, the combination of residential, commercial, community-facility, and forest damage, together with the documented casualty figures, mark the Sawyerville–Eoline tornado as one of the more destructive EF3 events of the April 27 outbreak in central Alabama.[1][2][9] The available NWS and database sources do not provide a specific monetary estimate of economic losses for this tornado, so the financial impact can only be inferred from the documented extent of structural and vegetation damage.[1][2][9]
