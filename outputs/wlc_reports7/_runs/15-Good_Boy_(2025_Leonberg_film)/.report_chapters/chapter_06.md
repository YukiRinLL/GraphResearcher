Good Boy attracted notable critical attention upon its festival premiere and in the lead-up to its theatrical release, with reviewers focusing on its formal gimmick of telling a haunted house story from a dogs perspective.[2][3][4][5][11][12] In a review for Variety, the film was described as a ghost story that uses the dog point-of-view device to find fresh angles within familiar genre territory, with particular praise for the way the camera work and blocking convey the animals experience of the haunting.[2] The reviewer noted that the film blends scares with humor and character beats rooted in the relationship between the dog and its human family.[2]

Screen Anarchys coverage from SXSW characterized Good Boy as a furry ghost hunter film and a haunted house chiller, highlighting its inventive use of canine perspective and its appeal to horror fans seeking a novel approach to ghost stories.[3] Genre outlet Rue Morgue, in reporting on the films poster and release date, framed the project as a horror title built around a dog who can see ghosts, emphasizing its high-concept premise.[4] Trailer coverage in IndieWire and The Hollywood Reporter similarly underscored the films dog-centric framing and suggested that the marketing positioned it as one of the more distinctive horror releases of the year.[11][12]

On the review aggregation site Rotten Tomatoes, Good Boy is listed with a dedicated page that compiles critical assessments of the film.[20] The page confirms its classification as a 2025 horror feature and collects reviews from festival and commercial release coverage, though specific score metrics may change as additional reviews are added over time.[20]
