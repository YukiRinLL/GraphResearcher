# Good Boy (2025 Leonberg film)

## Overview

Good Boy is a 2025 American horror film written and directed by Ben Leonberg. The film is associated with IFC Films and is distinguished by being told entirely from the point of view of a dog investigating a haunted house. The canine protagonist, portrayed by a dog named Indy/Indie, experiences and responds to supernatural phenomena while his human family explores a ghost-infested property. [1][2][3][4][5][8][9][10][11][12][14][16][17][19]

Good Boy premiered at the 2025 South by Southwest (SXSW) Film & TV Festival before receiving a wider theatrical release. IFC Films promoted and distributed the film, ultimately giving it a wide release in the United States on 3 October 2025 following significant online interest in the trailer. The film has attracted attention from trade publications and genre outlets for its formally unusual dog point-of-view concept and its blend of haunted house horror with offbeat humor. [1][2][3][4][5][8][9][10][11][12][14][16][17][19]

## Plot

Good Boy centers on a family who brings their dog to a reputedly haunted house, where the animal becomes the primary witness to the supernatural activity. The narrative is presented entirely from the dog’s visual and subjective perspective, including shots at the animal’s eye level and representations of how it perceives shadows, sounds, and apparitions. As the family explores the property, the dog reacts to spectral presences and potential threats, effectively serving as a ghost hunter whose instincts guide the audience through the unfolding haunting. [2][3][4][5][8][9][10][11][12][15]

The film combines tension and scares with moments of comedy derived from the dog’s behavior and its interactions with the human characters. Reviews and promotional materials describe the film as a haunted-house chiller in which the dog’s point of view reframes familiar ghost story elements, emphasizing sensory experiences such as smell and motion rather than dialogue-heavy exposition. The plot foregrounds the dog’s attempts to protect its owners while navigating confusing and threatening supernatural phenomena within the house. [2][3][4][5][8][9][10][11][12][15]

## Cast and characters

Good Boy features a central canine performance by Indy/Indie, who portrays the dog whose point of view structures the film. Promotional materials and interviews emphasize the dog’s role as the protagonist and point-of-view character, with the animal appearing in virtually every scene. [2][8][10][14][15][16][17][18][19]

Human cast and character details are documented in film databases, although they receive less emphasis in publicity than the dog. The principal human roles include members of the dog’s family, who accompany the animal into the haunted house and react to the unfolding supernatural events. Publicly available sources at the time of writing focus more on the dog’s performance and the technical challenge of filming from its perspective than on individual human character arcs. [2][8][10][14][15][16][17][18][19]

## Production

Good Boy was written and directed by Ben Leonberg, who developed the film around the conceit of telling a haunted house story entirely from a dog’s point of view. Leonberg has discussed the project as a long-term effort, describing in interviews and profile pieces how a small core team worked over several years to realize the technically demanding concept. According to a director profile, the film emerged from a multi-year collaboration in which a compact crew followed the dog performer through elaborate practical setups, emphasizing in-camera techniques rather than heavy reliance on visual effects. [2][3][4][5][8][9][11][12][14][15][16][17][18][19][21]

Cinematography for Good Boy is credited in public databases to Wade Grebnoel, a name that some sources indicate functions as a pseudonym associated with Ben Leonberg himself. Other listings simply present Wade Grebnoel as the director of photography without noting any connection to Leonberg, leaving the precise relationship between the credited cinematographer and the director somewhat opaque. Promotional materials for the film highlight the use of low-angle framing, handheld movement, and carefully choreographed blocking to simulate the way a dog would visually experience the haunted environment. [2][3][4][5][8][9][11][12][14][15][16][17][18][19][21]

Music for the film is attributed across sources to composer Sam Boase-Miller, with some materials also crediting Jeanmarie Riccobono. A professional profile for Boase-Miller lists Good Boy among projects on which he is credited as a composer, and indicates that the film involved multiple credited composers. This aligns with database entries that list Boase-Miller and Riccobono together as responsible for the score, even though certain summaries refer only to Boase-Miller, creating a minor discrepancy in how the music team is described. [2][3][4][5][8][9][11][12][14][15][16][17][18][19][21]

Good Boy was produced and promoted in partnership with IFC Films, with the independent label positioned prominently in marketing and release materials. The production makes extensive use of a real dog performer, Indy/Indie, whose training and on-set work are discussed in featurettes and interviews that emphasize the challenges of eliciting specific behaviors on cue while maintaining narrative continuity. Behind-the-scenes coverage highlights the intimate scale of the shoot, with accounts describing a compact core crew that contrasts with the longer formal credit lists found in public databases. [2][3][4][5][8][9][11][12][14][15][16][17][18][19][21]

## Release

Good Boy had its world premiere at the 2025 SXSW Film & TV Festival, where it screened in the festival’s program of narrative features. Festival listings identify the film as a 2025 production and situate it within the horror genre, emphasizing its dog point-of-view structure as a key selling point. Following the festival premiere, IFC Films promoted the film with an official trailer and a dedicated website ahead of its theatrical rollout. [1][2][3][4][5][6][8][9][10][11][12]

The official website and distributor materials present Good Boy as an IFC Films release dated 3 October 2025 in the United States. Trade coverage from IndieWire reports that, after the film’s trailer amassed more than one million views in four days, IFC expanded its plans and elected to give the film a wide theatrical release on that date. Promotional pieces highlight the film as a dog point-of-view haunted house horror title and frame the wide release as a response to strong online and genre-fan interest. [1][2][3][4][5][6][8][9][10][11][12]

Runtime information for Good Boy varies slightly between sources. IndieWire lists the film as running 72 minutes, while cinema listings such as the Gateway Film Center record its duration as 73 minutes. Both figures are used in public-facing materials, and no official clarification distinguishing between, for example, festival and theatrical cuts has been provided in the sources consulted. [1][2][3][4][5][6][8][9][10][11][12]

## Reception

Good Boy attracted notable critical attention upon its festival premiere and in the lead-up to its theatrical release, with reviewers focusing on its formal gimmick of telling a haunted house story from a dog’s perspective. In a review for Variety, the film was described as a ghost story that uses the dog point-of-view device to find fresh angles within familiar genre territory, with particular praise for the way the camera work and blocking convey the animal’s experience of the haunting. The reviewer noted that the film blends scares with humor and character beats rooted in the relationship between the dog and its human family. [2][3][4][5][11][12][13]

Screen Anarchy’s coverage from SXSW characterized Good Boy as a “furry ghost hunter” film and a “haunted house chiller”, highlighting its inventive use of canine perspective and its appeal to horror fans seeking a novel approach to ghost stories. Genre outlet Rue Morgue, in reporting on the film’s poster and release date, framed the project as a horror title built around a dog who can see ghosts, emphasizing its high-concept premise. Trailer coverage in IndieWire and The Hollywood Reporter similarly underscored the film’s dog-centric framing and suggested that the marketing positioned it as one of the more distinctive horror releases of the year. [2][3][4][5][11][12][13]

On the review aggregation site Rotten Tomatoes, Good Boy is listed with a dedicated page that compiles critical assessments of the film. The page confirms its classification as a 2025 horror feature and collects reviews from festival and commercial release coverage, though specific score metrics may change as additional reviews are added over time. [2][3][4][5][11][12][13]

Sources also diverge on how they credit the film’s cinematography. Some database entries and credit listings name Wade Grebnoel as the director of photography, with contextual information suggesting that this is a pseudonym associated with Ben Leonberg, whereas other listings simply credit Wade Grebnoel without any indication that the name functions as an alias. As a result, the exact relationship between Leonberg and the credited cinematographer cannot be conclusively established on the basis of the available sources. [1][2][3][4][5][6][8][10][11][14][16][18][19][20][21]

There is a spelling inconsistency in references to the dog performer, who is variously named “Indy” and “Indie” in different materials. While both sources clearly refer to the same canine actor, no definitive official spelling is established across all promotional and reference documents. Relatedly, one director profile describes Good Boy as having been made by a three-person team over the course of three years, emphasizing the compact nature of the core creative group, whereas public credit listings such as those found on film databases and catalogues list a larger number of crew members. This suggests that the “crew of three” description refers to a central production unit rather than the entirety of credited personnel, but the exact scope of that formulation is not specified. Music credits also show a minor inconsistency. Some materials and professional profiles indicate that composer Sam Boase-Miller worked on Good Boy alongside at least one additional composer, while certain listings attribute the score solely to Boase-Miller. A professional profile for Boase-Miller notes multiple composer credits on the film, aligning with database entries that list both Boase-Miller and Jeanmarie Riccobono, but not all public-facing descriptions of the film’s music team reflect this dual-credit situation.
