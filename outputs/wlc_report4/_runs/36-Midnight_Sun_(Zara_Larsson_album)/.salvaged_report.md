# Midnight Sun (Zara Larsson album)

## Lead

"Midnight Sun" is a studio album by Swedish singer Zara Larsson, released in 2025 through Epic Records and Sony Music. It follows her 2024 album "VENUS" and continues her work in upbeat dance-pop and electropop. The record was promoted as capturing the feeling of long Scandinavian summer days and nights, pairing high-energy pop production with lyrics about ambition, romance and emotional volatility. Upon release it received generally positive reviews from music critics for its energetic sound and Larsson's vocal performances, though at least one review criticized it as a formulaic attempt at summer pop. [1][2][3][4][11][24][25][28][15][16][17][18][33][34][36][38][39][40]

## Background and development

Before "Midnight Sun", Zara Larsson released several studio projects including "1" (2014), "So Good" (2017) and "VENUS" (2024), establishing herself as a mainstream pop artist with a focus on dance-pop and electronic-leaning production. [27][28][29][30]

Epic Records listed "Midnight Sun" as a 2025 CD album release by Zara Larsson, confirming its issue through the label and associating it with Sony Music's Epic imprint. [3][4][5][21][23][24]

A Sony Music Canada press release announced that Zara Larsson had released her new album "Midnight Sun", presenting it as a major pop project and part of her ongoing collaboration with Epic Records and Sony Music. [11][24]

In interviews around the album cycle, Larsson described the "Midnight Sun" era as tied to her long-standing ambition to be a leading pop artist, speaking about wanting the number-one spot and to leave an identifiable mark on pop history. [15][20][35]

A profile in The Fader quoted lyrics from the song "The Ambition" in which Larsson sings about wanting success so much that "everything's a competition", framing the album as a document of her drive and anxieties about pop stardom. [15][33][35]

## Composition and themes

Critics commonly describe "Midnight Sun" as a high-energy dance-pop album that rarely slows down, emphasizing uptempo beats, trance-inspired textures and glossy electronic production. [25][33][34][36]

Rolling Stone called the album a "high-energy dance-pop" record that "refuses to ever slow down" and sustains a summer atmosphere of "good vibes, long days, and late nights" across its running time. [33]

EUPHORIA. characterized the ten-track set as an "ultimate party record" with enough energy and zest to make listeners want to dance "anytime, anywhere". [34]

The album incorporates diverse influences including trance, Brazilian funk and ballroom, which reviewers link to Larsson's youthful energy and artistic growth. [16][34]

Several songs explore themes of ambition, self-confidence and emotional volatility; The Fader and Rolling Stone highlight "The Ambition" as a central track in which Larsson sings about wanting to be number one and feeling that everything is a competition. [15][33][35]

Rolling Stone groups songs such as "Midnight Sun", "Blue Moon", "Euro Summer" and "Puss Puss" as depicting sweetness and tender scenes like short skirts, late-night calls and lovers' handprints, while also noting darker or "messier" emotional moments where Larsson portrays jealousy and anger. [33]

The track "Pretty Ugly" is repeatedly singled out for its portrayal of female anger; Rolling Stone describes it as built on a stomp beat with lyrics about keying and crashing a car, while EUPHORIA. calls it an electro-influenced lead single that turns "female rage" into a powerful force. [33][34]

EUPHORIA. notes that across the album Larsson uses these sounds to represent her youthful energy, growth and ambition, with each song presented as an experience drawn from her own life. [34]

Melodic Mag writes that the album's trance-inspired production and Larsson's "hypnotizing" voice create a dreamy, sun-soaked atmosphere, describing the listening experience as being transported into a sunny dream. [36]

## Release and promotion

MusicBrainz lists "Midnight Sun" as a 2025 Zara Larsson release in the album category, grouping several physical and digital editions. [1][2]

Discogs documents multiple 2025 CD editions of "Midnight Sun", including standard and signed versions released through Epic Records, indicating a coordinated physical rollout. [3][4][5][21][23][24]

An Epic Records product page promotes the "Midnight Sun" CD with album artwork and purchasing options, aligning it with the label's roster of mainstream pop releases. [24]

Sony Music Canada's press release announcing the album frames it as a significant new release in Larsson's career and part of her partnership with Epic and Sony Music. [11]

Media coverage around the album's launch, including pieces in The Fader, i-D and other outlets, presented the "Midnight Sun" era as a pivotal moment in Larsson's ascent to international pop stardom. [15][19][20][35]

## Critical reception

"Midnight Sun" received generally positive reviews from music critics, with praise focusing on its high-energy pop production, summery atmosphere and Larsson's vocal performances, though some criticism targeted its formulaic tendencies and timing as a summer release. [25][33][34][36][38][39][40]

Rolling Stone's review calls the album a high-energy dance-pop record that keeps a summer mood of "good vibes, long days, and late nights", and concludes that Larsson is "coming into her own" on the project, with even its more formulaic moments shining brightly. [33][37]

EUPHORIA. describes "Midnight Sun" as "the ultimate party record" and highlights tracks such as "Pretty Ugly" and "Blue Moon" for their energetic, feel-good qualities, noting the album's diverse sonic palette and depiction of youth and ambition. [34]

Melodic Mag argues that "Midnight Sun" is Larsson's "best body of work yet", praising its trance-inspired production, cohesive mood and her "hypnotizing" vocals. [36]

An article on Beatroutemedia states that every song on the album feels carefully crafted, from its bright, loud production to the genuine, heartfelt emotions at its core. [40]

User-oriented platforms also reflect enthusiasm; a Musicboard review describes the record as a sleek, radiant dance-pop album that captures summer as both a season and a state of mind, and suggests it feels like the album Larsson was destined to make. [38]

In contrast, a review aggregated by Album of the Year criticizes "Midnight Sun" as "merely an attempt at a sultry summer pop album" released "about two months too late" and claims it misses every shot it takes, illustrating a more negative critical perspective. [39]

A feature in The Fader interprets the album as a key document of Larsson's ambition and desire for longevity in pop, arguing that her current ascent to stardom is closely tied to the artistic persona presented on "Midnight Sun". [15][35]
