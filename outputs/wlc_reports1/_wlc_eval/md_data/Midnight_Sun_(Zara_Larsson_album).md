# Midnight Sun (Zara Larsson album)

## Lead

Midnight Sun is a studio album by Swedish pop singer Zara Larsson, released on 26 September 2025 through the Sommer House and Epic Records imprints, with distribution by Sony Music Entertainment. It is a ten‑track dance‑pop record that has been described by critics as a high‑energy, Euro summer‑inspired album, and as a notable milestone in Larssons catalog. The album was followed by a remix and expanded counterpart, Midnight Sun: Girls Trip, released in 2026. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]

## Background and recording

Sony Music Canada announced Midnight Sun on 26 September 2025, describing it as Larssons hotly anticipated new album and a ten‑track set that ripples with personal confidence while combining kaleidoscopic pop and euphoric dance sounds. Prior to release, Larsson used social media to promote the project, sharing the track listing on TikTok with the caption Midnight Sun TRACKLIIIIST and noting that the album would be Out September 26, and posting on her verified Facebook page that Midnight Sun, the album would be released on that date. A fan‑run Zara Larsson wiki describes Midnight Sun as her fifth studio album and states that she announced the record on 5 June 2025 with a planned release date of 26 September 2025, although this characterization differs slightly from Genius, which refers to Midnight Sun as her fourth international album and fifth overall. [1][10][4][11][2]

Available sources provide limited detail about the recording sessions for Midnight Sun. Album credits compiled by Album of the Year list producers including Margo XS, MNEK, Zhone, ASAP P and Jon Shave, and identify songwriters such as Zara Larsson, MNEK, Helena Gao and Margo Wildman, suggesting a collaborative studio process centered on dance‑pop and electronic production teams. EUPHORIA. notes that Larsson later retained the production nucleus that shaped Midnight Sun for Midnight Sun: Girls Trip, bringing back MNEK, Margo XS, Helena Gao and Zhone, but it does not provide granular information about specific studios or recording dates for the original album. [5][9]

## Composition

Midnight Sun has been broadly categorized as a dance‑pop album that incorporates electronic dance music, Baltimore club, bubblegum bass, contemporary R&B and dancefloor drum and bass. Sony Music Canada describes the record as a ten‑track set that blends euphoric dance with kaleidoscopic pop and emphasizes personal confidence. Critics have frequently linked the albums sound and concept to the atmosphere of a European or Scandinavian summer, highlighting its focus on upbeat, high‑energy tracks and nocturnal themes. [5][1][6][7]

Rolling Stone describes Midnight Sun as Larssons fifth album and characterizes it as a high‑energy dance‑pop record that keeps the feelings of good vibes, long days, and late nights associated with Euro summer going across the track list. The title track, Midnight Sun, is described as deliriously hypnotic, with trance‑influenced production, a swirling chorus and an extended vocal run that together create a high‑energy kickoff for the album. BeatRoute similarly portrays the album as an homage to the Scandinavian summer, presenting a vibrant, unified world of endless daylight and euphoric dance music, and notes that the production is bright and loud across the songs. [6][7]

Individual tracks have been described as exploring themes of desire, guilt, empowerment, ambition and confusion. Rolling Stone highlights Girls Girl, in which Larsson is depicted as feeling guilty for wanting a childhood friends partner; Pretty Ugly, where she threatens slashed tires and crashed cars over a stomp beat; The Ambition, where she expresses a desire to be bigger, brighter; and Saturns Return, which addresses the uncertainties of adulthood and relinquishing control. BeatRoute describes Crush as a percussive pop track with a show‑stopping bridge that captures the anxiety and excitement of falling for someone who might be wrong, Pretty Ugly as a chaotic, rebellious cheerleader anthem rejecting expectations of being ladylike, Girls Girl as a portrayal of emotional chaos rooted in attraction to a friends boyfriend, and Eurosummer as a high‑energy dance song built around hypnotic accordion melodies. The same review characterizes Hot & Sexy as an assertive anthem introduced by a Tiffany Pollard soundbite, underscoring Larssons confident persona. [6][7]

## Release and promotion

Midnight Sun was released on 26 September 2025. Sony Music Canada issued a press release dated that day, stating that the multiplatinum singer had released her new album and that it was available at digital service providers via Sommer House and Epic Records. Album of the Year lists the same release date and formats the record as an LP issued on Sommer House and Epic. Larssons TikTok and Facebook posts publicized the release timeline in advance, with both posts indicating a 26 September release for Midnight Sun, the album. [1][5][10][4]

The Sony Music Canada announcement emphasizes that Midnight Sun includes new songs such as Blue Moon and Hot & Sexy alongside singles Pretty Ugly, Crush and Midnight Sun. It describes the album as reflecting Larssons personal confidence and positioning her within global pop, while encouraging listeners to get it at all digital service providers. The iHeartMedia press release for Midnight Sun: Girls Trip, issued ahead of the 2026 remix album, refers to Midnight Sun as Larssons 2025 breakthrough and frames Girls Trip as an opportunity to build upon the feeling of an endless summer night introduced by the original album. [1][8]

Beyond social media announcements and label press materials, publicly available sources do not provide a detailed chronology of promotional activities such as specific television appearances, radio campaigns or tour dates associated with Midnight Sun. EUPHORIA. notes that Larsson having already laid the groundwork with the now much‑discussed Midnight Sun subsequently invited listeners deeper into her world with Midnight Sun: Girls Trip, but this commentary focuses on the later project rather than enumerating the original albums promotional strategy. [8][9]

## Critical reception

Midnight Sun received generally positive reviews from music critics, though some responses were more mixed. Rolling Stone described the album as Larssons fifth and characterized it as a high‑energy dance‑pop LP that keeps the spirit of Euro summer alive, concluding that Larsson comes into her own across Midnight Sun and that even its more formulaic moments shine brighter than that Swedish summer sun. BeatRoute called Midnight Sun not just a regular pop record but a perfect example of how pop music should be made, comparing its impact to Katy Perrys Teenage Dream and praising it as a strong, cohesive album with no weak songs. [6][7]

Album of the Year summarizes Midnight Suns critical standing by reporting a critic score based on seven reviews and placing the record at number 224 out of 657 titles in its 2025 highest‑rated critics chart, alongside a user score based on 4,936 ratings that ranked the album at number 600 in a 2025 user chart. Pull quotes on the site include a remark from DIY describing Midnight Sun as near non‑stop and entirely sun‑drenched, exuberantly formulated pop scaffolded by MNEKs production style and Larssons Scandi‑pop‑meets‑dance direction, and a note from AllMusic calling the album a short and sweet catalog highlight that leaves listeners craving more of this endless bliss. Album of the Year also indicates that Midnight Sun appears in year‑end Best of 2025 lists associated with outlets such as DIY and Rolling Stone. [5]

BeatRoutes detailed review emphasizes the albums conceptual focus on Scandinavian summer and its unified sound, arguing that Larssons artistic growth is evident as she steps into her own and abandons trend‑chasing in favor of a distinctive aesthetic. The reviewer highlights tracks such as Crush, described as a standout percussive pop jam, and Pretty Ugly, characterized as a chaotic, rebellious cheerleader anthem. A user review on Musicboard similarly states that Midnight Sun feels like the album Larsson was destined to make, describing it as a sleek, radiant dance‑pop record that captures summer as more than just a season. [7][12]

Other commentary has been more reserved. Pitchfork, in a review primarily focused on the remix album Midnight Sun: Girls Trip, retroactively assigns the original Midnight Sun a semi‑canonical score of 6.9, noting that the album did not initially make waves and was not particularly newsworthy at release, especially in the United States. The reviewer reports that they initially found the title track catchy but considered the other songs weaker and remarks on the timing of issuing an album featuring the hook This feels like Eurosummer in late September. An Instagram reviewer posting as marieltalkspop describes the album as plain, writing that all the songs sound similar and that only Pretty Ugly and Eurosummer stood out positively, while the rest were seen as forgettable and lacking creativity. A discussion thread on the r/popheads subreddit includes a user who conversely characterizes Midnight Sun as cohesive and polished and claims that it has more personality than any of Larssons previous releases. [13][14][15]

EUPHORIA. refers to Midnight Sun as a now much‑discussed record that successfully pushed Larsson back into the spotlight of the global pop industry, contrasting with Pitchforks assessment that the album did not initially make waves. These differing interpretations illustrate the range of critical and fan responses to Midnight Suns commercial and cultural impact, with some sources emphasizing its role as a star‑making, spotlight‑returning project and others framing it as a solid but less transformative entry in Larssons discography. [9][13][5][7][14][15]

The press release also mentions Blue Moon and Hot & Sexy as new songs appearing on the record. Pitchfork notes that the music video for Midnight Sun combined a nihilistic dolphin meme aesthetic with a vintage screensaver style, but does not supply further production details such as directors or release dates, and other sources surveyed do not elaborate on the visual promotion of the album. ## Personnel

Detailed personnel credits for Midnight Sun have not been fully reproduced in the accessible sources, but Album of the Year lists key contributors, identifying Margo XS, MNEK, Zhone, ASAP P and Jon Shave as producers and naming Zara Larsson, MNEK, Helena Gao, Margo Wildman and Zhone among the songwriters. EUPHORIA. further notes that MNEK, Margo XS, Helena Gao and Zhone constituted the production nucleus retained for the subsequent project Midnight Sun: Girls Trip, implying their central role in shaping the sound of the original album. ## Conflicting descriptions

Several sources differ in their descriptions of Midnight Suns place in Zara Larssons discography and its impact. The fan‑run Zara Larsson wiki refers to Midnight Sun as her fifth studio album, while the Genius album page describes it as her fourth international album and fifth overall, reflecting varying criteria for counting international versus overall releases and differentiating studio albums from other types. EUPHORIA. characterizes Midnight Sun as a record that successfully pushed Larsson back into the spotlight of the global pop industry, whereas Pitchfork states that the album did not initially make waves, particularly in the United States, and retroactively gives it a semi‑canonical score of 6.9. Critical opinions also vary, with outlets such as BeatRoute and DIY presenting Midnight Sun as an exemplary, cohesive pop album, while the Instagram reviewer marieltalkspop describes it as plain and lacking creativity. [11][2][9][13][5][7][14]
