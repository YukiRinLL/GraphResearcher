## “The Rector of Justin” novel 1964 Louis Auchincloss publication details plot summary characters

- ISBN 10: 0618224890  ISBN 13: 9780618224890  Publisher: Harper Perennial, 2002 [1]

- Regarded as one of Louis Auchincloss's most accomplished novels, THE RECTOR OF JUSTIN centers on Frank Prescott, the founder of an exclusive school for boys. Eighty years of his life unfold through the observations of six narrators, each with a unique perspective on the man, his motivations, and the roots of his triumphs and failings. [1]

- "September 10, 1939. I have always wanted to keep a journal, but whenever I am about to start one, I am dissuaded by the idea that it is too late. … I shall be an instructor of English at Justin Martyr, an Episcopal boys' boarding school thirty miles west of Boston." [1]

- A CRUCIBLE COVERED WITH IVY; THE RECTOR OF JUSTIN. By Louis Auchincloss. 341 pp. Boston: Houghton Mifflin Company. $4.95. [2]

- The New York Times obituary of Louis Auchincloss, who died in 2010, said, "'The Rector of Justin' (1964), which was a best seller and a finalist for the National Book Award, is regarded by many critics as Mr. Auchincloss’s best and most important novel. Its protagonist is Frank Prescott, the headmaster of a New England boys’ school before the war, a man of intellect and idealism who could be noble, generous and kind but also, by turns, cruel, callous and arbitrary." [3]

- At one point the Prescott character in the book says, "The older I get the more I realize that the only thing a teacher has to go on is that rare spark in a boy’s eye." [3]

- Later in The Rector of Justin, the Prescott character says, "There is no real distinction between the pulpit and the classroom. I tried to put God into every book and sport in Justin. That was my ideal, to spread a sense of his presence so that it would not be confined to prayers and sacred studies and to spread it in such a way as to make the school joyful." [3]

- Elsewhere in the book, Prescott claims, "the greatness of the private school…is not that it produces geniuses—they grow anyway, and can’t be made—but that it can sometimes turn a third-rate student into a second-rate one." [3]

- Louis Auchincloss was the author of more than sixty books of fiction in a writing career that spanned the second half of the twentieth century and the opening years of the twenty-first. He was also an estate lawyer for a sizable portion of that time and a denizen of a privileged sphere of New York society. [4]

- Yet among his extensive body of work there remain standout performances. Auchincloss himself told George Plimpton that he would like to be remembered on the strength of three books, Portrait in Brownstone, House of Five Talents, and The Rector of Justin, which was a finalist for the National Book Award in 1965. It lost out, deservedly, to Saul Bellow’s Herzog. [4]

- The rector of Justin is Reverend Francis Prescott, D.D., founder and headmaster of Justin Martyr, a school modeled on Auchincloss’s years as a student at Groton. [4]

- He is a great man in the eyes of his new hire, a young master by the name of Brian Aspinwall, who provides early impressions via his journal. Aspinwall is undeniably callow, and wracked with self-doubt, but he views Prescott as a pole star of sorts, a man whose example he will never be able to credibly follow, but whose very existence justifies his own efforts to live as a man of faith and guide his young charges as ably as possible. [4]

- Charley Strong, a former Justin Martyr who eventually ends up in a romantic relationship with Prescott’s daughter, writes in the aftermath of his World War I service that, "He it was who baptized and confirmed me, he who talked to me of my doubts and miseries, he who gave me a love that made the shallow, prattling love of shallow, prattling parents seem like the spray on one’s face in a speedboat at sea." [4]

- Early on, Aspinwall notes that he would like to use his newly started journal to keep a record of Prescott’s last years at Justin Martyr. Gradually the idea emerges – not of his own accord – that he should write Prescott’s life. He consults Horace Havistock’s account of a youth spent as Prescott’s great friend, but this is prior to David Griscam’s conclusion that, since Prescott won’t cooperate with his idea to write the headmaster’s life, Aspinwall should do so. [4]

- The Rector of Justin centers on Reverend Frank (Francis) Prescott, D.D., founder and headmaster of Justin Martyr, an exclusive Episcopal boys’ boarding school in New England, located about thirty miles west of Boston. [1][3][4]

- The narrative of The Rector of Justin unfolds over approximately eighty years of Frank Prescott’s life through the perspectives of six different narrators, each offering a distinct view of his character and motivations. [1]

- One of the narrators in The Rector of Justin is Brian Aspinwall, a twenty‑seven‑year‑old newly appointed English master at Justin Martyr, who begins a journal on September 10, 1939 to record his impressions of Prescott and initially intends it as a record of Prescott’s last years at the school. [1][4]

- Through plot developments in The Rector of Justin, Brian Aspinwall’s initial plan to chronicle Prescott’s final years in his journal evolves into the task of writing Prescott’s life, influenced by Horace Havistock’s account of Prescott’s youth and by David Griscam’s suggestion after Prescott refuses to cooperate with Griscam’s own biographical project. [4]

- Charley Strong is a former student of Justin Martyr who later becomes romantically involved with Prescott’s daughter and serves as one of the narrative voices reflecting on Prescott’s role in his spiritual development. [4]

- Frank Prescott, the rector in The Rector of Justin, is portrayed as a complex figure—an intellectual and idealist who can be noble, generous, and kind, yet also cruel, callous, and arbitrary in his role as headmaster of the boys’ school. [3][4]

- Within The Rector of Justin, Frank Prescott articulates an educational philosophy that emphasizes realistic limits of schooling by asserting that the greatness of a private school lies not in producing geniuses but in sometimes raising a third‑rate student to second‑rate, and that a teacher’s only reliable guide is the rare spark in a boy’s eye. [3]

- The Rector of Justin presents religion as integral to education at Justin Martyr, with Frank Prescott declaring that there is no real distinction between pulpit and classroom and that he sought to infuse a sense of God’s presence into every book and sport so that religion would permeate school life and make the school joyful. [3]

- Louis Auchincloss, an estate lawyer and member of elite New York society, wrote more than sixty works of fiction, and he indicated to George Plimpton that among his books he most wished to be remembered for Portrait in Brownstone, House of Five Talents, and The Rector of Justin. [4]

## “The Rector of Justin” novel study guide analysis book summary teaching guide

- The GradeSaver community note summary describes *The Rector of Justin* as following the life and career of Frank Prescott, the influential headmaster (rector) of the prestigious Justin Martyr School. The narrative begins with Brian Aspinwall, a former student of Justin Martyr, who reflects on his experiences and on Prescott’s impact; Aspinwall serves as narrator, giving insight into Prescott’s character and formative events. [7]

- Prescott is portrayed as founding Justin Martyr School in the early 20th century and transforming it from a struggling institution into a highly respected New England preparatory school. His dedication to discipline, intellectual precision, and moral guidance earns admiration from students, faculty, and parents. [7]

- The text mentions Prescott’s wife Harriet, who struggles with her role as headmaster’s wife and often feels overshadowed by his devotion to the school. Tension between his family life—he has three daughters—and his commitment to Justin Martyr creates conflict. [7]

- Prescott’s interactions with students show his teaching methods and his influence as a mentor and father figure, shaping their lives and guiding them toward success. At the same time, his strictness and unwavering commitment to tradition create controversy and clashes with some students and faculty. [7]

- The summary indicates that the narrative jumps forward over time, highlighting major moments in Prescott’s tenure and the changing social landscape. It cites world events such as the Great Depression and World War II as affecting the school and community. [7]

- One question asks why Prescott is so unwavering in building a boys’ boarding school. The answer explains that Prescott was educated at a boys’ boarding school founded by Horace, a religious fanatic who instilled discipline and inspired his students. Prescott became close to Horace and promised to follow in his footsteps by establishing his own boys’ boarding school. [8]

- Cordelia is depicted as rebellious and bold, frequently challenging her father when she disagrees. She is angered by his decision to focus on a boys’ school rather than programs to empower his daughters and other girls, viewing this as favoritism toward men. The commentary notes that Cordelia even speculates her father might be gay because his emotional focus seems to be on men and boys rather than women. [8]

- A third question discusses why the school board forces Prescott to retire as headmaster. The answer stresses Prescott’s strong attachment to the Episcopal school he built with his own resources and has led since its founding. However, at age 82, his efficiency has declined, in movement and thinking, raising concerns among the board. The board, having authority to remove the head, compels his retirement so that a younger, more energetic man named David (described as taking over the position) can lead the school. [8]
