## “Ring of Nestor” gold ring archaeology discovery context excavation publication

- “The many years of research that led to the present paper on the so-called Ring of Nestor (CMS VI, nο. 277), with the documentation of its authenticity, have offered me the opportunity to discuss the cases of erroneous identifications of some Creto-Mycenaean gold rings as fakes… This tendency, underpinned by the obscure – even legendary – circumstances in which these rings were found, not only the Ring of Nestor but also the Ring of Minos, has been reassessed completely in favour of their authenticity. This revision has been significantly reinforced by recent finds of iconographic subjects of comparable resonance… as well as by more thorough study of the techniques and craftsmanship of these unique signet rings of the first centuries of the Late Bronze Age (16th-15th century BC).” [1]

- “The Ring of Nestor, measuring 3.29 cm by 2.18 cm, is the fifth largest Aegean signet ring.” [1]

- “The key pictorial unit of the Ring of Nestor examined in this paper is Evans’s ‘watch-dog’ or ‘dragon’, namely the guardian of the old ‘tree of the world’, whose branches host the most populous scene in Aegean glyptic art. This motif is here identified as a scorpion, a major apotropaic symbol in Bronze Αge Aegean iconography, which kills the ‘Μinoan Dragon’ of the ring and offers a sound interpretation of the ring’s iconography.” [1]

- “In 1950, Martin Nilsson revoked, in the second edition of his ‘Minoan-Mycenaean Religion,’ his initially unwary approach to the object as an authentic Bronze Age artifact and now discussed it, among other examples, as a ‘suspect object.’… In 1954, Hagen Biesantz took a decisive stand against the ‘Ring of Nestor’s’ authenticity primarily based on the observation of stylistic oddities. Agni Xenaki-Sakellariou extensively discussed some of the ring’s iconographical details she regarded suspicious in 1994 and critically commented on its typological and technical features. Ingo Pini’s article on the ‘Ring of Nestor’, written in 1998, can be read as a reply to this, for he referred explicitly to Xenaki-Sakellariou’s arguments, countering them by naming new insights and evidence in support of the ring’s authenticity.” [2]

- “This paper presents an analysis of the iconography surrounding the 'Ring of Nestor' in the context of Aegean forgeries and authenticity debates… The connection between incurved altars and the ring's depiction supports its authenticity. … Generations of scholars have debated the genuineness of both Nestor's and Minos' rings.” [3]

- “The authenticity of the Ring of Nestor has been debated, primarily focusing on its iconography and the possibility of forgery by the Gilliérons. … New technical observations presented in this document support the ring's authenticity, countering previous arguments against it. The ring showcases unique stylistic and manufacturing details that align with known Minoan-Mycenaean artifacts, reinforcing its ancient origin.” [4]
