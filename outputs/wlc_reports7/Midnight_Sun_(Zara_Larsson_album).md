## "Midnight Sun" Zara Larsson album review

- “Euro summer isn’t over if you don’t want it to be. Zara Larsson’s fifth album, Midnight Sun, is an unbroken rush of high-energy dance-pop joy that refuses to ever slow down.” [4]

- Rolling Stone notes that Larsson is “celebrating the sun-drenched energy of a summer in her home country” and that the album helps keep “the good vibes, long days, and late nights going.” [4]

- On the title track, Rolling Stone writes: “‘Midnight Sun’ is deliriously hypnotic, with notes of trance in its swirling chorus uplifted by Larsson’s extended run.” [4]

- Rolling Stone groups “Blue Moon,” “Euro Summer,” and “Puss Puss” as songs that “traffic in the sweetness,” mentioning “tender scenes of short sundresses, late-night phone calls and handprints left on her ass.” [4]

- Describing “Girl’s Girl,” Rolling Stone notes Larsson “feels guilty for wanting a childhood friend’s man,” and in “Pretty Ugly” she sings over a stomp beat and “threatens slashed tires and crashed cars” while asking, “Have you ever seen a pretty girl get ugly like this?” [4]

- Rolling Stone compares “The Ambition” to “the raw moments of Charli XCX’s Brat,” quoting the lyric: “But that’s the thing with the ambition / I want it so much / Everything’s a competition / So out of touch,” and calling it “one of the biggest earworm moments of the project.” [4]

- On “Saturn’s Return,” Rolling Stone writes that Larsson “leans into life’s confusions, giving up control she sought when she was younger,” highlighting it as a standout. [4]

- Rolling Stone concludes: “Those messier moments are where Larsson comes alive. You can hear her coming into her own across Midnight Sun, where even her more formulaic moments still shine brighter than that Swedish summer sun. That ambition is paying off.” [4]

- DIY’s excerpt via Album of the Year: “Near non-stop, Midnight Sun is entirely sun-drenched, exuberantly formulated pop that's both scaffolded by MNEK's production style and the Scandi-pop-meets-dance future that Zara has readily paved.” [5]

- Beatroutemedia states: “Zara Larsson’s new album isn’t just a regular pop record; it’s a perfect example of how pop music should be made, and I haven’t been this hooked on an album’s sheer, great pop sound since Katy Perry’s Teenage Dream.” [6]

- Beatroutemedia describes the album as “a strong, cohesive record with no weak songs, where every song feels carefully crafted, from the bright, loud production to the genuine, heartfelt feelings at its core.” [6]

- On style, Beatroutemedia writes that Midnight Sun is “an homage to the Scandinavian summer, giving us a vibrant, unified world of endless daylight and euphoric dance music,” and “a fun, maximalist fever dream of electro-pop that never loses sight of its heart.” [6]

- Beatroutemedia on "Midnight Sun" and "Blue Moon": the opening pair is called "pure fire," with the title track offering “soaring vocals over trance-inspired production” and “Blue Moon” delivering “a club-ready beat to celebrate that perfect romantic high.” [6]

- On "Girl’s Girl," Beatroutemedia notes that it “brilliantly flips the feminist expectation of female loyalty on its head,” portraying “the horrifying, guilt-ridden feeling of crushing on a friend’s boyfriend.” [6]

- Beatroutemedia calls “Crush” “arguably the highlight of the entire project,” describing it as “an irresistible percussive pop jam with an amazing, show-stopping bridge” that captures “the anxiety and excitement of falling for someone totally wrong for you.” [6]

- For "Eurosummer," Beatroutemedia writes that it is “a total sonic shift,” a “rousing, high-energy dance beat built around hypnotic accordion melodies,” and “the ultimate party track” that “instantly transports you to a dizzying European holiday.” [6]

- Beatroutemedia describes “Hot & Sexy” as “a bold and confident, unavoidable declaration” that opens with “the iconic, commanding sound bite from reality TV legend Tiffany Pollard,” and suggests it cements Zara’s control over the pop scene. [6]
