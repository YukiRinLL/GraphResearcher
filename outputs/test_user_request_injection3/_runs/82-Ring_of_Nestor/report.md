- The Griffin Warrior tomb was discovered in May 2015 near the Palace of Nestor at Pylos during renewed University of Cincinnati excavations. [1][2][3][4][5]

- The tomb is dated to Late Helladic IIA, roughly 1450-1400 BCE, and contained the remains of a single male in his 30s described as a warrior. [1][2][4][5]

- The burial included four gold signet rings decorated with Minoan ritual scenes or iconography. [1][2][3][4][5]

- The rings are tentatively interpreted as probably originating in Crete and as having been used to seal documents or objects. [4]

- Researchers argue that the ring imagery and related Minoan grave goods indicate substantial Mycenaean engagement with Minoan religious iconography and cultural exchange. [1][2][3][4][5]

- The Pylos Combat Agate is described as a Cretan Late Minoan I sealstone found in the Griffin Warrior tomb near the warrior's right arm, and its combat scene draws on earlier mainland and Neopalatial Cretan iconography. [3][5]
