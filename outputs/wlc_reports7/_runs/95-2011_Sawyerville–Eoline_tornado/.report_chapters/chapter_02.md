The Sawyerville–Eoline tornado formed within a broader episode of severe convective storms on 27 April 2011, when a powerful storm system produced numerous tornadoes across Alabama and surrounding states.[1][9] The NWS Birmingham office’s event overview for the "Historic Outbreak of April 27, 2011" documents a series of tornadoes in central Alabama on that date, including long-track, strong, and violent tornadoes associated with supercell thunderstorms.[9] Within this sequence, the Sawyerville–Eoline tornado is catalogued as an EF3 tornado affecting Greene, Hale, and Bibb Counties, with an extended track and substantial casualty figures.[1][2][9]

According to the NWS damage survey specific to the event, the tornado touched down in far southwestern Greene County near the Tombigbee River just west of Tishabee, then moved northeast across rural portions of the county north of Forkland.[2] The survey reports that the tornado subsequently crossed the Black Warrior River into Hale County, west of Sawyerville, and intensified as it approached and crossed Alabama Route 14 north of Sawyerville, reaching EF3 intensity with estimated winds of about 145 mph (233 km/h).[2] As the tornado progressed through Hale County, it crossed local roads including County Roads 18, 21, and 29 and Alabama Highway 69, producing extensive structural damage and numerous casualties.[2]

Continuing northeastward, the tornado entered the Talladega National Forest near the community of Ingram and tracked toward the Bibb County line, where it caused widespread tree damage consistent with its strong intensity.[2] In Bibb County, the tornado moved through the Eoline area, where the NWS survey describes significant structural damage characteristic of EF3 intensity, with winds again estimated near 145 mph.[2] In Eoline, numerous mobile homes and single-family residences sustained minor to major damage, a dozen such structures were destroyed, and community facilities including the Eoline Fire Department and a nearby business were demolished.[2] The survey notes that the tornado weakened slightly to EF2 intensity with estimated winds near 125 mph (201 km/h) as it crossed Alabama Highway 5 south of West Blocton; several additional mobile homes and homes were heavily damaged or destroyed near County Road 26, and thousands of trees were downed along the later portion of the path before the tornado dissipated just northeast of Marvel.[2]

The Alabama Tornado Database for 2011 and the NWS event overview both list the Sawyerville–Eoline tornado with a total path length of approximately 72.13 mi and a maximum width of about 1,760 yd, underscoring its status as one of the longer-track tornadoes of that day in central Alabama.[1][2][9] The tabular database provides the tornado’s date, time, counties affected, EF rating, path statistics, and casualty totals, while the narrative survey adds detailed geographic and intensity information along the track.[1][2]
