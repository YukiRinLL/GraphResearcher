The Sawyerville–Eoline tornado occurred during the late-April 2011 tornado outbreak, often referred to as the 2011 Super Outbreak, which affected large portions of the central and eastern United States.[6][8] Outbreak overviews from Grokipedia and Tornado Talk describe a multi-day sequence of severe thunderstorms and tornadoes from 25 to 28 April 2011, with 27 April representing the single most active and deadly day of the event.[6][8] Both sources identify Alabama as one of the states hardest hit by strong and violent tornadoes on 27 April, with numerous long-track tornadoes causing widespread destruction and hundreds of fatalities.[6][8] Within Alabama, the NWS Birmingham "Historic Outbreak of April 27, 2011" page similarly documents many tornadoes of EF2 intensity or greater, including the Sawyerville–Eoline EF3 tornado in Greene, Hale, and Bibb Counties.[9]

Although Grokipedia and Tornado Talk agree on the exceptional severity of the 2011 Super Outbreak, their summary statistics differ slightly. Grokipedia reports that the outbreak produced about 362 tornadoes in total, whereas the Tornado Talk overview gives a figure of about 368 tornadoes, reflecting differences in data sources or counting methodology.[6][8] For 27 April specifically, Grokipedia cites approximately 199 tornadoes, while Tornado Talk estimates about 224 tornadoes for that day.[6][8] The two sources also report slightly different overall fatality totals, with Grokipedia giving about 321 deaths associated with the outbreak and Tornado Talk listing about 324 fatalities.[6][8] Despite these minor discrepancies, both accounts consistently portray the outbreak as one of the most significant tornado events in U.S. history and emphasize the major role of Alabama tornadoes, including the Sawyerville–Eoline storm, in contributing to the high casualty and damage figures.[6][8][9]

The NWS Birmingham outbreak summary for 27 April 2011 provides a more localized operational perspective, listing the Sawyerville–Eoline tornado alongside other significant Alabama tornadoes in a tabular format that includes time, affected counties, EF rating, path length, maximum width, and casualty statistics.[9] This official record aligns with the Alabama Tornado Database for 2011 in identifying the Sawyerville–Eoline tornado as an EF3 event with a long track across three counties, seven fatalities, and fifty injuries, situating it among the more destructive but not highest-rated tornadoes (which included several EF4 and EF5 tornadoes) during the outbreak in central Alabama.[1][2][9]
