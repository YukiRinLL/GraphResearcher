- 该章节通过对金属工艺、浮雕技法和图像传统的比较分析，得出结论认为“Ring of Nestor”的现有证据更支持其真实性，并反驳早期提出的近代伪作假说。 [8]

- Vlachopoulos 将戒指印面划分为包含船或舟、树或圣树、祭坛与供品、岩石或贝特尔石以及女性人物或女神等图像区域，用以讨论弥诺斯宗教空间和神祇显现场景。 [8]

- 该章节包含对“Ring of Nestor”收藏史与现存位置的梳理，叙述其从发现到纳入博物馆收藏体系的过程，并讨论涉及的法律和出处问题。 [8]

- 《Traces of Places》一文将“Ring of Nestor”用作弥诺斯金戒指上缩微圣地场景的例证，指出其图像中可能包含座位、祭坛和树木，并与其他戒指上类似组合进行对比。 [25]

- 论文提出弥诺斯金戒指构成“便携圣地”，佩戴者通过戒指上组合神祇、建筑、自然元素和祭祀设施的图像，象征性携带或再现特定圣地，“Ring of Nestor”被纳入这一解释。 [25]

- “Fakes and Dubitanda”章节描述“Ring of Nestor”印面图像为在船上的女性人物、祭坛、圣树与贝特尔石等主题的组合，并指出这种高密度与“选集式”拼贴被视为质疑其真实性的证据。 [26]

- 该章节在回顾工艺和风格证据后，主张尽管“Ring of Nestor”存在诸多疑点，但在当前证据下不应轻率地将其归为伪作。 [26]

- 讨论 Isopata 戒指的论文在分析神祇显现叙事模式时，多次以“Ring of Nestor”为平行例证，强调其图像中女性人物、船只、祭坛和树木的组合可被理解为宗教仪式与神祇临在的场景。 [6]

- 该论文引用埃文斯等早期出版物，包括对“Ring of Nestor”印面的绘图和说明，指出这些早期图像资料构成后续对戒指图像学解读的重要基础。 [6]

- The Ring of Nestor is described in scholarship as a gold signet ring with a seal face measuring about 3.29 x 2.18 cm, and as unusually large among known Aegean gold signet rings. [8]

- Scholarly descriptions of the Ring of Nestor's seal image identify a multi-element scene that includes a ship or boat, trees or a sacred tree, an altar with offerings, and a female figure or deity; some analyses also include a rock or betyl element. [1][8][25][26][6]

- The Ring of Nestor is interpreted in scholarship as a miniature sacred-site or portable-sanctuary image that stages religious ritual and divine presence, while its dense and seemingly curated composition has also been cited by some authors as evidence for doubts about authenticity. [1][8][25][26]

- The same bibliography includes a section on “Aegean and Cypro‑Aegean Non‑Sphragistic Decorated Gold Finger Rings” and lists works on Minoan and Mycenaean gold rings, placing the Ring of Nestor within this thematic grouping. [4]

- The abstract of Pini’s article notes that “In the past the authenticity of the so‑called Ring of Nestor has been debated mostly in terms of iconography. Arguments against its ancient origin rest …” and that the study reassesses these arguments. [3]

- Secondary descriptions of Pini’s article indicate that it “belongs to a group of Late Bronze Age Aegean gold signet rings,” and that Pini compares the Ring of Nestor’s iconography and workmanship with securely provenanced Minoan and Mycenaean pieces to evaluate its authenticity. [3]

- The preprint’s abstract states that it “presents an analysis of the iconography surrounding the ‘Ring of Nestor’ in the context of Aegean forgeries and authenticity debates.” [1]

- According to the summary, the preprint situates the ring within “Late Bronze Age Aegean glyptic motifs,” comparing its scene and figure types to other rings with processional or cult imagery from Minoan and Mycenaean contexts. [1]

- The preprint discusses “the ‘Minoan dragon,’ tree and underworld or afterlife elements seen on the ring,” relating these motifs to religious symbolism and to questions of cultural attribution and chronological placement. [1]

- Palmer writes that “The ‘Ring of Minos’ (AM 1938.1110) and ‘Ring of Nestor’ (VI.277) have both been denounced as fakes by Nilsson (1950: 42–49) and Biesantz (1954⁷: 115–119) …”. [29]

- In Palmer’s catalogue the Ring of Nestor appears as “VI.277” in the Corpus der Minoischen und Mykenischen Siegel (CMS). [29]

- Palmer situates the Ring of Nestor within a broader pattern of “composite creatures on seals and sealings,” using its hybrid imagery and composition to discuss religious symbolism and stylistic phases of the Late Bronze Age Aegean. [29]

- The chapter states: “On the Ring of Nestor, a Minoan Dragon can also be seen in a natural setting. It is standing on a grassy ground in front of the trunk of the tree whose branches …”. [30]

- Within its discussion of hybrid creatures, the chapter identifies the hybrid animal on the Ring of Nestor as a “Minoan Dragon” and considers it alongside examples from Knossos and mainland contexts. [30]

- In the bibliography of the metal study appears the entry: “‘The Ring of Nestor’: A glimpse into the Minoan after‑world and a sepulchral treasure of gold signet‑rings and bead‑seals from Thisbê, Boeotia. JHS 45, 1–75.” [31]

- The cited title characterizes the material as “a sepulchral treasure of gold signet‑rings and bead‑seals from Thisbê, Boeotia” and frames the Ring of Nestor as offering “a glimpse into the Minoan after‑world.” [31]

- Scholarly analyses generally situate the Ring of Nestor within the repertoire of Late Bronze Age Aegean gold signet rings and glyptic, comparing its iconography and workmanship with Minoan and Mycenaean rings. [4][3][1]

- The Ring of Nestor’s imagery, including a hybrid creature identified as a ‘Minoan dragon’, a tree, and possible underworld or afterlife elements, is interpreted by several scholars within a Minoan religious or afterlife symbolic context. [1][29][30][31]

- The Ring of Nestor has been the subject of authenticity debates, with mid‑20th‑century scholars such as Nilsson and Biesantz denouncing it as a fake, and later studies (e.g., Pini 1998 and subsequent iconographic preprints) reassessing these arguments and treating it within the corpus of Late Bronze Age Aegean gold signet rings. [3][1][29]

- Modern scholarship often treats the Ring of Nestor under the standard Aegean glyptic corpus Corpus der Minoischen und Mykenischen Siegel (CMS) as entry VI.277, indicating its working classification among Minoan–Mycenaean seals and rings despite ongoing authenticity and cultural attribution debates. [29]

- The Ring of Nestor has been connected, at least in one major study, with a sepulchral treasure of gold signet‑rings and bead‑seals from Thisbê in Boeotia, implying a claimed funerary association and mainland find context for the material discussed. [31]
