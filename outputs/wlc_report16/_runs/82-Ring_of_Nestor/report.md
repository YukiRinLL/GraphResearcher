- Summary table on the file page: "English:  The 'Ring of Nestor', displayed in the Ashmolean Museum, Oxford, in 2025" with date "10 August 2025" and source "Ashmolean Museum, Oxford". [1]

- ResearchGate snippet: "Here new technical observations are presented which serve to support the authenticity of the Ring of Nestor. In 1995 the ring was once again placed on display in the Ashmolean Museum." [2]

- Heidelberg record: "Evans, Arthur J. 'The ring of Nestor'. A glimpse into the Minoan after-world and a sepulchral treasure of gold signet-rings and bead-seals from Thisbê, Boeotia. London, 1925." [3]

- Cambridge Core extract: "The first subject of the present communication is a truly royal treasure of gold rings and of bead-seals in the same metal found in a Mycenaean rock tomb near the site of Thisbê in Boeotia. The find was made in 1915, at a time when war conditions diverted the course of discovery from official channels, and a fortunate chain of circumstances now enables me not only to describe but to exhibit to the Society the whole hoard." [4]

- The artifact known as the "Ring of Nestor" has been displayed at the Ashmolean Museum in Oxford in at least two documented years, 1995 and 2025. [2]

- The Ring of Nestor originates from a sepulchral treasure of gold signet-rings and bead-seals discovered in 1915 in a Mycenaean rock tomb near Thisbê in Boeotia. [3][4]
