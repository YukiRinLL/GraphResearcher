- “具身智能技术路径整体可分为四条，包括模块化分层、分层大模型、端到端大模型和世界模型，如图5 所示。” [8]

- “模块化分层路线沿用传统机器人技术体系，通过感知、定位建图、路径规划、运动控制等模块化设计与分层架构，提升各层算法性能。” [8]

- “分层大模型路线在模块化架构基础上，引入大模型作为高层认知中枢，负责任务理解、策略规划等高层决策，通过标准接口与下层控制模块协同。” [8]

- “端到端大模型路线尝试弱化模块边界，构建从多模态输入到动作输出的统一模型，实现‘感知－决策－执行’的一体化。” [8]

- “世界模型路线通过学习环境动态的内部表征，为策略学习和规划提供模型支撑，可与分层或端到端控制结合，形成混合技术体系。” [8]

- “端到端 VLA 尚未形成‘感知－决策－执行’的完整闭环，还无法支撑真实环境应用。一是 泛化性有限……另外，现有 VLA 模型性能表现与本体强相关，在跨本体迁移时很难复现模型性能。 二是 长程任务执行能力不足。” [8]

- “在具身智能决策中，目前主要有两类技术路线：一类是层次化决策……另一类是端到端视觉‑语言‑动作模型（VLA）……前者兼顾工程可控性与语义理解能力……后者在大规模数据驱动下具备更强的场景泛化潜力，但在稳定性和可解释性方面仍存在明显不足。” [9]

- “世界模型作为具身智能的一种关键技术路径，通过显式建模环境状态和动态，可以在内部模拟环境中进行规划和策略学习，提高样本效率并支持长视野决策。” [9]

- “特别指出，生成式人工智能，尤其是大语言模型、多模态大模型以及正在演进的‘信息 ‒ 物理 ‒ 认知’三域融合大模型等技术在加速具身智能演进中的关键作用。” [10]

- Recent surveys and reports on embodied AI commonly categorize high‑level technical routes into modular layered control, hierarchical large‑model (LLM/MLLM‑centric) control, end‑to‑end large‑model (VLA/robot‑Transformer) control, and world‑model‑driven routes, with growing attention to their joint or hybrid combinations. [8][11][12]

- In large‑model‑empowered embodied decision making, two decision paradigms are repeatedly identified as mainstream: hierarchical decision‑making (LLM/MLLM‑based high‑level planning plus low‑level controllers) and end‑to‑end Vision‑Language‑Action (VLA) decision‑making that maps multimodal inputs directly to actions. [9][11]

- The modular layered route in embodied AI/robotics follows the traditional robotics stack, decomposing systems into perception, localization and mapping, path planning, and motion control modules, improving each layer separately but facing bottlenecks such as limited data closed‑loops and high cross‑scenario migration costs. [8]

- The hierarchical large‑model route augments a modular control architecture by using a large language or multimodal model as a high‑level cognitive hub for task understanding and strategy planning, coordinating with lower‑level controllers via standard interfaces, but faces challenges in model–robot coupling, real‑time and safety requirements, and engineering complexity. [8]

- The end‑to‑end large‑model route seeks to weaken hand‑crafted module boundaries by training a unified multimodal model (typically a VLA or robot‑Transformer) that directly maps perception and language inputs to actions, forming an integrated perception–decision–execution loop, but current VLA systems still suffer from limited out‑of‑distribution generalization, poor long‑horizon task execution, strong embodiment‑specific coupling, and instability and limited interpretability. [8][9][11]

- World‑model‑driven embodied learning is treated as a key technical route in embodied AI, in which an internal model learns environment state and dynamics to enable future prediction and counterfactual simulation, supporting long‑horizon planning, safer policy evaluation, and improved sample efficiency, and it can be combined with hierarchical or end‑to‑end controllers in hybrid systems. [8][9][11][12][13][14]

- Surveys on embodied world models classify internal world‑model techniques into categories such as video‑generation‑based models, models integrating 3D reconstruction, and latent world models with compressed representations, and further analyze them along axes like generation modality (e.g., image/video, 3D geometry, semantic state), control signals (e.g., action‑conditioned, language‑conditioned, mixed), and generation viewpoint (e.g., first‑person vs third‑person). [13][14][15]

- At the system level, recent work distinguishes three primary functional roles for embodied world models in robotics: (1) cloud‑side data synthesis engines that generate simulation data to train perception and VLA models, (2) environment agents or evaluators in simulators for training and benchmarking embodied agents, and (3) on‑device ‘brains’ providing real‑time state inference and prediction on robots. [13][14]

- A high‑level line of work frames embodied AI routes as LLM‑driven versus world‑model‑driven: LLM/MLLM‑driven routes emphasize semantic reasoning, task decomposition, and natural language interaction but lack strong physical constraints and future imagination, whereas WM‑driven routes emphasize physically plausible multi‑step prediction, long‑horizon planning, and safety verification but are relatively weak in semantic understanding and task abstraction; joint MLLM–WM architectures are proposed to combine these complementary strengths. [12]

- In large‑model‑empowered embodied decision‑making, hierarchical decision routes are characterized as more controllable and suitable for safety‑critical industrial scenarios, while end‑to‑end VLA routes show stronger potential for scene generalization given large‑scale data but currently lag in stability and interpretability. [9][11]

- Generative AI models—including large language models, multimodal large models, and emerging information–physical–cognitive ‘three‑domain fusion’ large models—are regarded in recent trend reports as key enabling technologies for embodied intelligence, with the three‑domain fusion large model proposed as an important future technical route that unifies information‑domain data, physical‑domain states, and cognitive‑domain task intentions within a single model. [10]

- Up‑to‑date embodied AI paper lists and surveys classify research not only by technical routes but also by task and resource categories such as datasets, embodied perception, embodied navigation, embodied question answering, embodied manipulation, and simulation platforms/environments, illustrating that route design is intertwined with specific embodied tasks. [16]

## 针对代表性公司或机构，总结其具身智能相关的技术路径与核心方案，包括模型范式、训练数据与方法、以及硬件/机器人形态。

- RT-2 is built by taking a vision-language model trained on internet-scale data (web images and text) and then fine-tuning it on robot data so the same model can be used for robot control. [32]

- The model outputs discrete tokenized actions that control a robot arm to perform manipulation tasks such as picking up objects based on high-level instructions (including reasoning tasks like 'pick the extinct animal'). [32]

- RT-2 builds on a Vision-Language Model (VLM), similar to PaLI-X / PaLM-E style architectures, and adds an action output head so that the model can map from images and text to low-level actions for a real robot. [33]

- RT-2 is trained in two stages: (1) large-scale pretraining on web data (image-text pairs, web documents) to learn semantic and visual representations, and (2) fine-tuning on a dataset of robot trajectories collected via teleoperation on a real robot performing manipulation tasks in a kitchen-like environment. [33]

- The training data for the robotics part is collected on a single type of robot (a 7-DoF arm on a wheeled mobile base) executing hundreds of manipulation tasks; the web data is drawn from large internal corpora. [33]

- The resulting dataset is described as the largest open-source real robot dataset to date, consisting of hundreds of thousands of trajectories covering 160k+ tasks and more than 500k skills. [34]

- Open X-Embodiment instead aggregates datasets from 22 different robots across 21 institutions, standardizing them in a unified format (RLDS episodes). [34]

- The paper states that Open X-Embodiment assembles a dataset from 22 different robots, sourced through a collaboration of 21 institutions, demonstrating 527 skills over 160,266 tasks. [35]

- On top of this dataset they train RT-X models: RT-1-X (an image+text to action transformer controller) and RT-2-X (a vision-language-action model). These are large transformer models that output parameterized actions in Cartesian space (end-effector pose deltas and gripper commands). [35]

- The press release announces that Boston Dynamics and TRI have demonstrated a Large Behavior Model (LBM) controlling the Atlas humanoid robot. The LBM is described as a single neural network that directly commands the entire robot, including legs and arms, enabling long, continuous sequences of tasks combining whole-body locomotion and manipulation. [36]

- Boston Dynamics describes its work with TRI building Large Behavior Models (LBMs) for the Atlas humanoid. The blog states they are building 'end-to-end language-conditioned policies' that enable Atlas to execute long-horizon manipulation tasks using full-body motions like stepping, crouching, shifting center of mass, and avoiding self-collisions. [37]

- The architecture is a diffusion transformer trained with a flow matching loss; it predicts future action trajectories rather than single-step actions. [37]

- Agility Robotics explains its development of a 'whole-body control foundation model' for the Digit humanoid robot. The foundation model is described as a relatively small LSTM neural network with fewer than one million parameters. [38]

- It is trained entirely in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, a physics simulator, for 'decades of simulated time over three or four days' to track whole-body motion. [38]

- NVIDIA announces Project GR00T, described as a 'foundation model for humanoid robots' intended to accelerate the development of general-purpose humanoids. GR00T is positioned as a vision-language-action model that will allow humanoid robots to understand natural language and emulate and learn from human demonstrations. [39]

- The article explains that teams can use GR00T with a data pipeline called GR00T Blueprint, involving stages like teleoperation to collect seed data, MimicGen to generate synthetic variations of trajectories, and large-scale simulation rollouts for training. [40]

- GR00T N1 leverages a combination of web video, synthetic trajectories from simulation, and real robot data, organized in a 'data pyramid', to learn generalizable policies. [40]

- 1X uses NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab to generate photorealistic environments for training and evaluating tasks in simulation, including parallel simulations to train reinforcement learning controllers for locomotion and foundation capabilities. [41]

- Training of robot foundation models is done on NVIDIA HGX B200 GPU clusters, using both simulated and real-world robot data. [41]

- TRI announces a generative AI approach called 'Diffusion Policy' that allows robots to quickly learn new, dexterous skills. The press release explains that diffusion models—used widely in image generation—are adapted to learn robot policies: they take sensor observations and generate future action trajectories. [42]

- The training data consists of human demonstrations collected via VR teleoperation of TRI’s mobile manipulation robots in realistic home-like environments. With a few minutes of demonstration data, the diffusion policy can learn tasks like unstacking plates, pouring liquids, and manipulating deformable objects. [42]

- The Mobile ALOHA project describes a low-cost whole-body teleoperation system that enables collection of bimanual mobile manipulation data. The hardware platform consists of a differential-drive mobile base with a torso and two low-cost robotic arms (a mobile manipulator), equipped with cameras. [43]

- A human operator wears a VR-like suit and uses handheld controllers to teleoperate the robot, creating demonstrations of tasks such as cooking, tidying, and other household chores. [43]

- The core learning approach is supervised behavior cloning: a neural network policy is trained to map observations (RGB images, robot state, and sometimes language instructions) to low-level control commands recorded from the teleoperation. [43]

- The agreement’s stated purpose is to develop 'next generation AI models for humanoid robots,' enabling them to understand and generate language, perceive surroundings, and perform tasks in the physical world. [44]

- Toyota Research Institute (TRI) develops general-purpose mobile manipulation robots and Large Behavior Models using generative diffusion policies trained from VR teleoperated demonstrations in realistic home- and factory-like environments, where task-specific diffusion models take multimodal observations and output future action trajectories for wheeled-base mobile manipulators with multi-DoF arms and grippers. [36][37][41][45][42][46]

- Boston Dynamics and TRI’s Atlas Large Behavior Model (LBM) is an end-to-end, language-conditioned diffusion transformer that maps RGB images, proprioception, and language prompts to full-body action trajectories at 30 Hz, directly commanding a 50+ DoF Atlas humanoid for long-horizon tasks combining locomotion and manipulation, trained primarily from teleoperated demonstrations that include perturbed and failure cases collected both in simulation and on real hardware. [36][37]

- Google DeepMind’s RT-2 is a transformer-based vision-language-action model built by repurposing a PaLI-X/PaLM-E-style web-scale vision-language model and adding an action output head, then training it in two stages—large-scale pretraining on internet image–text and document data followed by fine-tuning on teleoperated trajectories from a single 7-DoF arm on a wheeled mobile base in kitchen-like environments—so that it can map tokenized language and visual inputs directly to discrete Cartesian end-effector and gripper commands for a mobile manipulator. [32][33]

- The Open X-Embodiment project aggregates real-robot datasets from 22 robots across 21 institutions into a unified RLDS-format corpus of roughly 160k tasks and over 500k skills, primarily collected via teleoperation and scripted policies on industrial arms, collaborative arms, and mobile manipulators, and uses this corpus to train RT-1-X and RT-2-X transformer policies that map images and text to parameterized Cartesian end-effector deltas and gripper commands, yielding cross-robot policies with positive transfer compared to single-robot training. [34][35]

- The Mobile ALOHA project uses a low-cost mobile manipulator with a differential-drive base, torso, and two arms, teleoperated via a VR-like suit and handheld controllers to collect bimanual household-task demonstrations; a supervised behavior cloning policy then maps RGB images, robot state, and optional language inputs to low-level control commands, and co-training with static ALOHA arm-only datasets improves performance. [43]

- Agility Robotics’ Digit whole-body control foundation model is a sub–one-million-parameter LSTM that receives high-level end-effector objectives and outputs low-level control signals to keep the bipedal Digit humanoid balanced while executing logistics-oriented tasks; it is trained entirely in NVIDIA Isaac Sim over decades of simulated time and transfers zero-shot to real hardware, operating as a low-level motor layer under higher-level task-specific policies, while reinforcement learning in Isaac Sim and real deployment data from industrial customers are used to refine higher-level behaviors. [38][47]

- NVIDIA’s Project GR00T is a vision-language-action foundation model for bipedal factory and logistics humanoids that aims to serve as a general AI brain across multiple embodiments, using embodiment-aware encodings and deployment on robots such as 1X NEO and Fourier GR-1 with Jetson/IGX compute, and is trained via the GR00T Blueprint pipeline that builds a data pyramid by combining teleoperated seed demonstrations, MimicGen-generated trajectory variations, large-scale Isaac Lab/Isaac Sim rollouts, web video, and real robot data. [39][40]

- 1X Technologies trains robot foundation models and world models for its NEO humanoid using NVIDIA Isaac Sim/Lab to generate photorealistic physics-based simulations and parallel reinforcement-learning rollouts, and trains these models on NVIDIA HGX B200 GPU clusters using a mix of simulated and real robot data, then deploys multimodal neural networks onboard NEO using NVIDIA Jetson Thor for real-time perception and decision-making. [41]

- Figure AI develops human-sized bipedal humanoid robots (Figure 01/02/03) for industrial and future domestic applications and reportedly employs vision-language-action models, including partner foundation models from OpenAI, so that its robots can interpret natural language and plan actions; a 2024 collaboration agreement with OpenAI and Microsoft targets next-generation AI models for humanoid control and language understanding, using training data from simulated environments and real-world pilot deployments with teleoperated and autonomous attempts logged for offline training. [48][49][44]

- OpenAI is reported to be expanding hiring in robotics, hardware, simulation, and multimodal data roles with the inferred goal of building foundation models that unify language, vision, and action for robot control using both simulation-generated and real robot data across humanoid and other platforms, though explicit architectural details have not been disclosed. [48]
